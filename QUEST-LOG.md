# Quest Log

Every exercise in the course as a checklist, in F2P route order. The app tracks these too (and gives XP).

## Capstone: build your own F2P game

- [ ] **1. Pitch it in one breath** (after L02, L04) — Pick ONE F2P mobile concept: genre + a twist + who it's for. Write its one-sheet: title, genre, target player, platforms, age rating, the core loop in one sentence, 3 unique selling points, a monetization hypothesis and 2–3 competitor games.
  - Deliverable: A one-page one-sheet (doc, Notion or a photo of paper).
- [ ] **2. Loop it** (after F2P1) — Draw your core loop (what you do every 30 seconds), session loop (one 3–5 minute session) and meta loop (what pulls you back for weeks). Mark where the player feels progress and where each retention hook sits.
  - Deliverable: One loop diagram plus one short paragraph per loop.
- [ ] **3. Reward economy v0** (after L13, F2P1) — List every currency (soft, hard, energy, tokens), every source and every sink, and design the reward schedule for days 1–7. Simulate a player's balance for the first week in a spreadsheet.
  - Deliverable: Economy sheet: sources/sinks table + day-by-day balance for 7 days.
- [ ] **4. The first ten levels** (after L09, L12) — Make a beat chart for the FTUE and levels 1–10: what each level teaches (one new mechanic or hazard at a time), its difficulty, the rest beats, and where the first real difficulty spike lands (and why there).
  - Deliverable: Beat chart table + a rough difficulty curve sketch.
- [ ] **5. Screens & thumbs** (after L07, L08) — Wireframe your home/lobby screen and in-game HUD for a portrait phone. Mark thumb zones, touch-target sizes, currency bars, notification dots and the path to the store. Map every touch gesture to an action.
  - Deliverable: 2 wireframes (paper photos are fine) + a one-table control map.
- [ ] **6. Cast & conflict** (after L05, L10, L11) — Create your hero's character sheet (personality, silhouette, metrics), 3 enemies with distinct behaviors and 1 boss, using the enemy and boss templates from the Bonus Levels.
  - Deliverable: 1 character sheet, 3 enemy cards, 1 boss card.
- [ ] **7. Go live** (after L14, F2P2) — Design one social feature (clan, co-op or PvP), a 30-day LiveOps calendar, and a store page with 3 offers (prices + why). Run an ethics check: which of your hooks could feel predatory, and how do you defuse it?
  - Deliverable: Calendar, store mock-up and a one-page feature spec.
- [ ] **8. Ship the pitch** (after L17, BX) — Write the ten-pager (use the F2P-adapted template in the Bonus Levels) and a 10-slide pitch deck. Post it publicly for feedback: r/gamedesign, a Discord, X or LinkedIn. Public feedback is the boss fight.
  - Deliverable: Ten-pager + pitch deck + a link to your public post.

## 🕹️ Press Start — Press Start — Foreword, Author, Intro & Acknowledgments

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Napkin spec** (10 min) — Pick a feature from a F2P game you play (e.g. a daily login calendar) and explain it in one drawing with at most 5 labels. No paragraphs.
- [ ] **Everything-is-gameplay hunt** (10 min) — Screenshot 5 non-gameplay screens in a F2P game (shop, settings, loading, popup, out-of-lives) and write one line each on how it changes what the player feels or does.
- [ ] **Box test** (5 min) — Write your favorite game's objective in one sentence, as if printed on a board-game box.
- [ ] **First-page autopsy** (10 min) — Install a new F2P game and log what happens in the first 30 seconds: spectacle hook, calm tutorial, or straight to play? Did it make you want 'page two'?
- [ ] **Party up** (10 min) — List 3 people who could be your first test audience and 1 possible mentor or community. Message one of them.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The Cartoon Spec** (60 min) — Turn one real F2P feature (event, shop offer, clan feature) into a one-page visual spec: one-line goal, player fantasy, 4-6 panel flow, 3 key numbers, target KPI, 2 open questions.
  - Deliverable: One PNG/PDF page.
  - Done when: Someone who never played the game can explain the feature back to you after 60 seconds of looking. Bonus: post it publicly as Day 1.
- [ ] **Launch your swipe file** (60 min) — Create a design-journal template (Game, Genre, Core loop in one sentence, Best thing, Worst thing, What I'd steal, Monetization notes) and fill it for 3 F2P games from different genres.
  - Deliverable: Template plus 3 filled entries.
  - Done when: Each entry has a one-sentence core loop and at least one 'what I'd steal'.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Block Blast!** (10 min) — Look for: The single core action and the feedback (sound, animation, combo text) that makes it satisfying. → If you removed everything except the core action, would it still be fun? Why?
- [ ] **Royal Match** (10 min) — Look for: What the fail screen offers when you run out of moves (extra moves, coins, ad, retry). → How does this 'non-gameplay' screen try to keep you playing, and is it fair?
- [ ] **Any newly released F2P game** (10 min) — Look for: The store page and the first minute of play. → Does the opening hook honestly match the game you actually play by minute 5?

## 🐣 Level 1 — Welcome, N00bs!

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Box test x5** (10 min) — Write one-sentence 'board-game box' objectives for 5 F2P games. Flag any that need more than 12 words as Mouse Traps.
- [ ] **P-R-V audit** (10 min) — Pick a LiveOps event in a game you play. Write its player requirement, 3 rules, victory condition and failure state. Is the victory condition clear in the UI?
- [ ] **Genre DNA** (10 min) — Write 3 top-grossing mobile games as hybrid formulas: 'Book genre A + Book genre B + meta C'.
- [ ] **Ancestor hunt** (10 min) — Find one mobile game's oldest ancestor in the book's history (arcade, Game & Watch, PC strategy...) and write what it inherited.
- [ ] **Role spotting** (10 min) — Open a mobile studio's credits or careers page. Count the book roles vs modern-only roles (LiveOps, UA, data, monetization).
- [ ] **Fix the feedback** (5 min) — Rewrite 'this level sucks' into a designer-grade critique with cause, evidence and a suggested fix.
- [ ] **Hand ball remix** (5 min) — Invent a one-player game with one everyday object: 2 rules, 1 victory condition, 1 failure state. Play it for 2 minutes.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The Genre Lineage Poster** (90 min) — Map 12 current mobile games onto mobile genre families, connect each to its book ancestor genre, and tag each as ad-driven, hybrid or IAP-driven. Circle one empty spot where a new hybrid could exist and pitch it in one sentence.
  - Deliverable: One poster image (Figma, Miro, Canva or paper).
  - Done when: All 12 games placed with ancestor lines and monetization tags, plus one circled gap with a one-sentence hybrid pitch. Bonus: post it publicly.
- [ ] **Org chart of a hit** (75 min) — Design the team for a hypothetical 30-person F2P match-3 studio after global launch: every role with headcount, what they own and the one metric they watch. Then trace your own event idea through the team in 8-12 steps.
  - Deliverable: One-page org chart + feature flow.
  - Done when: Headcount totals 30, every role has an owner metric, and the flow includes PM, LiveOps, economy, level design, QA, data and UA steps.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match** (10 min) — Look for: The rules, victory condition, failure state, and what's offered at failure across 3 levels. → Can you state each level's objective in under 8 words, and how does the game show it on screen?
- [ ] **Clash of Clans** (10 min) — Look for: The four X's (expand, explore, exploit, exterminate) in the first sessions. → Which X appears first and which last, and why might the designers order them that way?
- [ ] **Subway Surfers** (10 min) — Look for: Platformer DNA: mascot, jumps and dodges, readable hazards. → What did the runner keep from platformers, and what did it drop to fit a one-thumb phone game?
- [ ] **Any top-grossing F2P game** (10 min) — Look for: Which parts came from UA/creative (store screenshots, video), LiveOps (current event) and monetization (first offer). → Which team role would you most want on this game, and why?

## 📝 Level 4 — You Can Design a Game, But Can You Do the Paperwork?

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Recipe card** (10 min) — Write your own analogy for making games (not chili, not fishing) with at least five mapped steps, including crunch and polish.
- [ ] **USP detox** (10 min) — Open the store page of a top-grossing F2P game. Rewrite its screenshot captions as 5 USPs, delete any filler like 'amazing graphics', and mark which ones you could show in a 15-second ad.
- [ ] **Font genre quiz** (5 min) — Mock up the fake title 'Gem Kingdom' in three fonts that signal three different genres, then ask a friend to guess the genre of each.
- [ ] **Clockwork undead drill** (10 min) — Take three vague words from any design doc ('reward', 'enemy', 'event') and replace each with a specific term or definition nobody could misread.
- [ ] **Rating detective** (10 min) — Look up one F2P game's rating on Google Play and on the App Store. List the descriptors (in-app purchases, random items, user interaction) and explain why you think it got that tier.
- [ ] **Rule of Threes rewrite** (5 min) — Take any list of five or more examples you've written and cut it to the best three: one that sets up the idea, one that contrasts, and one that rounds it out.
- [ ] **Mini beat chart diff** (15 min) — Make two columns for levels 1 and 2 of a mobile game you play (goal, new mechanic, new blocker, booster, reward, theme). Mark what's new in level 2, then check for clumping or samey-ness.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The F2P One-Sheet + Feature One-Pager (design-test simulation)** (120 min) — Pick a real F2P game you know well. Write a one-page F2P One-Sheet for it (all 12 fields), as if you were pitching it before it existed. Then write a feature one-pager for a new LiveOps event for that game, with a mini reward table and a three-step player flow diagram.
  - Deliverable: Two one-page PDFs: the F2P One-Sheet and the feature one-pager.
  - Done when: Each doc is exactly one page; three successful comps; 3–5 showable, non-filler USPs; the one-pager has a primary KPI plus two guardrails, a target segment, an economy line, an ethics check, at least two edge cases and a note on age-rating impact. Bonus: post it publicly and ask for one piece of feedback.
- [ ] **The F2P beat chart** (90 min) — In Google Sheets, build a 20-level progression sheet for a hypothetical puzzle game: one row per level with goal type, moves, new mechanic or blocker, booster unlocked, difficulty tier, reward, theme and music track, plus a coin column compared against booster prices.
  - Deliverable: A shared spreadsheet plus three sentences on what you'd change after seeing win-rate data.
  - Done when: No level after level 1 introduces more than one new blocker; goal types and themes alternate (no more than three identical goals in a row); there's a difficulty sawtooth; every new blocker arrives with its counter; the player can afford a booster about every 3–5 levels.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match** (10 min) — Look for: When each new blocker or booster first appears in the early levels, and whether it comes with a tutorial or a free sample. → Does it follow Rogers' '2–3 new things per level' and 'introduce with the counter' rules, or go even slower? Why might mobile go slower?
- [ ] **Brawl Stars** (10 min) — Look for: Features on a new or low-level account's home screen that are visible but locked. → Which of Rogers' five progression starts is this, and what does showing locked features do for motivation?
- [ ] **Monopoly GO!** (10 min) — Look for: Every event running right now, with its timer, theme and reward type. → Sketch a one-week LiveOps beat chart from what you see. Is there clumping or samey-ness, and how do they keep it from feeling repetitive?
- [ ] **Any top-10 grossing F2P game (store page)** (10 min) — Look for: The age rating, descriptors, screenshot captions and the first lines of the description. → What are its 'five USPs', which three comps would you put on its one-sheet, and which rating did it design toward?

## 💎 DLC 1 — F2P Core: Loops, Progression & Economy

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Loop Trio** (15 min) — Draw the core loop of 3 games you've played (one casual, one midcore, one hybrid-casual) as 4-box diagrams (Action → Reward → Invest → New challenge), each with a one-sentence summary.
- [ ] **Napkin LTV** (10 min) — A game has ARPDAU $0.20, its average player is active 7 days in their lifetime, and CPI is $1.20. Is it profitable? Then list 3 design changes that would widen the margin. (LTV ≈ $1.40: thin but positive.)
- [ ] **Notification Audit** (10 min) — Check your phone's recent game notifications. Classify each as timer, social, event, win-back or guilt-trip. Which would you keep, rewrite or kill?
- [ ] **Tier List** (10 min) — Sort 6 games on your phone into hyper-casual / hybrid-casual / casual / midcore / hardcore and note each one's main revenue model (ads, IAP, hybrid).
- [ ] **Sixty-Second Storyboard** (15 min) — Storyboard the first 60 seconds of an imaginary F2P game in 6 panels: no login wall, show the fantasy, a juicy first action, a first win.
- [ ] **Faucets & Drains** (10 min) — For one game you play, list every way you earn its soft currency and every way you spend it. Is anything piling up in your wallet? What does that suggest about its economy?
- [ ] **Know Thyself** (15 min) — Take Quantic Foundry's free Gamer Motivation Profile online. Write your top 3 motivations, one F2P game that fits them, and one that doesn't (and why).
- [ ] **Saw-Tooth Hunt** (15 min) — Play 15 levels of a puzzle game, rating each level's difficulty 1–5 (attempts + feeling). Sketch the curve. Do you see ramps, spikes and breathers?

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The Full Teardown (7 days)** (300 min) — Pick a F2P game you've never played (e.g. Royal Match, Hay Day, Survivor.io, Marvel Snap). Follow the 14-step teardown and the template across 7 days: recorded FTUE on day 1, short daily sessions with a notification/offer log, synthesis on day 7.
  - Deliverable: A 2–6 page teardown: FTUE log, core + meta loop diagrams, economy sources/sinks table, monetization summary, notifications log, motivation profile, and at least 5 hypotheses ("They do X → to move KPI Y → because Z").
  - Done when: Every template section is filled; at least 2 diagrams; 3 strengths, 3 weaknesses, 1 "steal this", 1 "I'd change this"; a short summary posted publicly (thread, blog or portfolio).
- [ ] **The Tiny Economy Spreadsheet** (120 min) — Design a mini economy for a hypothetical game: 1 soft currency, 1 hard currency, 3 sources, 4 sinks, a 10-level upgrade track. Model 30 days for a casual free player (2 sessions/day), an engaged free player (5 sessions/day) and a light payer (engaged + one $4.99 pack on day 3).
  - Deliverable: A spreadsheet with income per day, cost per level, cumulative balance and days-to-milestone per archetype, a balance-over-time chart, and a paragraph on where walls appear and how you smoothed them.
  - Done when: Every source maps to at least one sink; no archetype is stuck more than ~3 days on any step in the first two weeks (or the wall is intentional and explained); the payer is ahead but the free path reaches level 10 within 30 days.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match** (10 min) — Look for: Fresh install with a stopwatch: first win, first star spent on a task, first booster taught, first offer, first notification request. → At what timestamps did the core loop and the meta loop each "click", and what was taught before it was needed?
- [ ] **Clash Royale** (10 min) — Look for: What you earn after a win, what you can spend it on, and what (if anything) limits progress in one sitting. → Draw the core loop and name the session cap or exit hook. What would bring you back in 3 hours?
- [ ] **Hay Day** (10 min) — Look for: Every currency and timer; what you produce, what orders ask for, what diamonds are offered for. → List 3 faucets and 3 drains for coins. Where is time being used as a currency?
- [ ] **Survivor.io** (10 min) — Look for: In-run skill choices, out-of-run upgrades, energy costs, and every rewarded-ad offer. → Which loop keeps a run exciting, which keeps you coming back tomorrow, and where do ads appear as a fair trade?

## ⚡ Level 13 — Now You're Playing with Power — Power-ups & Rewards

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Booster spec sheet** (10 min) — Write Rogers' 7-question power-up spec, plus acquisition and price/sink role, for a new match-3 booster.
- [ ] **D-O-M-G sort** (10 min) — List 12 power-ups or boosters from mobile games you play and sort them into Defensive, Offensive, Movement and Game changer.
- [ ] **Fanfare audit** (5 min) — Beat one level in a mobile game and count the praise layers (sound, animation, VFX, text, voice). Compare to Zak & Wiki's 8.
- [ ] **Shop aisles** (10 min) — Map every item in one F2P shop to Rogers' ten shopkeeper categories. Which aisles are missing, and why?
- [ ] **Achievement writer** (10 min) — Write 8 clever, funny achievements for a merge or farming game, each nudging players toward a mechanic they might not try.
- [ ] **Robot chicken hunt** (5 min) — Find the most 'me too' cosmetic in a multiplayer mobile game and write 3 reasons people want it.
- [ ] **Ethics flip** (15 min) — Take three reward features from a game you play and write a 'delight' version and a 'manipulation' version of each.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **Mini F2P economy + reward plan** (120 min) — For a hypothetical match-3 or merge game, define soft and hard currency (names, icons, denominations); build a sources & sinks spreadsheet with day 1/7/30 balances; design a 7-day login calendar, a fixed-ratio level-chest schedule, one variable reward with odds and a pity rule, and a 30-tier free+premium season pass; spec 3 pre-level and 3 in-level boosters with free supply rates; finish with an ethics review against the delight vs manipulation table.
  - Deliverable: A spreadsheet plus a one-page summary.
  - Done when: Day-30 balances show neither runaway inflation nor starvation, every booster has a free source, every random reward has published odds and a pity cap, each element cites the Rogers rule it follows, and you've posted the summary publicly asking a F2P designer for one critique.
- [ ] **Reward-loop teardown** (75 min) — Pick one F2P game and document every reward a player gets in their first 3 days: what, when, which of Rogers' 10 reward types, which schedule (fixed/variable ratio/interval), and how much fanfare. Flag anything that crosses the ethics line.
  - Deliverable: A timeline table plus a 5-sentence verdict.
  - Done when: At least 20 rewards are logged with type, schedule and fanfare rating, and the verdict names the game's strongest and weakest reward practice.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match or Candy Crush Saga** (10 min) — Look for: Every way boosters arrive (earned, gifted, bought) and how pre-level boosters are offered. → Do boosters read as help or ransom? Where's the free supply?
- [ ] **Brawl Stars or Clash Royale** (10 min) — Look for: Pass track visibility, daily shop rotation, rarity tiers, cosmetics other players can see. → Which Rogers reward rules (show early, reward often, two great choices, 'me too') can you spot?
- [ ] **Monopoly GO! or Coin Master** (10 min) — Look for: The collection album, set rewards, duplicate handling and trading. → How does it turn Rogers' trophy shelf into a retention loop, and where's the variable-ratio reward?
- [ ] **Genshin Impact or Honkai: Star Rail** (10 min) — Look for: The banner page, trial characters, odds disclosure and pity info (no spending needed). → Map the banner to Half-Life's hear → see → watch → wield funnel. Is the pity system clear?

## ⚙️ Level 12 — The Nuts and Bolts of Mechanics

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Platform Primer remix** (10 min) — Pick 6 platform types from Rogers' primer and turn each into a match-3 blocker or merge obstacle with the same behavior.
- [ ] **The crate ban** (5 min) — Write 15 breakable objects for a cozy farming F2P game. No crates, barrels or chests allowed.
- [ ] **Tell me when** (10 min) — Invent one timing hazard for a runner and list three tells (visual, audio, environmental) that warn the player.
- [ ] **Prop storm** (10 min) — Brainstorm props for a bakery (easy), a pirate cove (medium) and a space-station laundromat (hard). Stop when it gets silly.
- [ ] **Checkpoint audit** (10 min) — Fail a level in any mobile game and grade its respawn/continue against FACE · SAFE · GROUND · FLAT · SAVED.
- [ ] **Microgame anything** (15 min) — Turn an everyday chore (watering plants, parallel parking, folding laundry) into a 30-second microgame using Rogers' minigame rules.
- [ ] **Rock-on-the-head hunt** (5 min) — List three unfair deaths or fails you've suffered in mobile games and redesign each to be fair.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **Orchestrate a 10-level episode** (90 min) — Design a 10-level match-3 or merge episode using the 7-step orchestration. Invent 2 new mechanics (one helpful, one blocker) and 1 'enemy' (spreading blocker or countdown bomb). For each level write the goal, elements, what's new and the intended feeling. Mark spikes, relief levels and the final twist, and justify your hardest level against 'no rocks on the head'.
  - Deliverable: A one-page level table (spreadsheet or markdown) plus a sketch of one board.
  - Done when: All 7 orchestration steps map to at least one level, no level introduces two new things at once, the hardest level has a written fairness check, and you've posted it publicly asking for one piece of feedback.
- [ ] **Timer teardown** (60 min) — Pick one timer-heavy F2P game (Clash of Clans, Hay Day or Township). Catalog every timer: its length, whether it's in-level or meta, what it paces, whether it can be skipped and for how much. Then rate each on clarity (shows time left?) and fairness (would it exist without paid skips?).
  - Deliverable: A timer spreadsheet with a 5-sentence verdict on where the game sits between 'pacing' and 'toll booth'.
  - Done when: At least 8 timers are cataloged with clarity and fairness ratings, and your verdict cites at least two of Rogers' rules.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match or Candy Crush Saga** (10 min) — Look for: How a new blocker is introduced: alone first? tutorial hand? repeated? combined later? → Which of Rogers' 7 orchestration steps can you spot across those levels?
- [ ] **Subway Surfers** (10 min) — Look for: Obstacle tells, timing windows, and what happens after a crash (the continue offer). → Does the respawn follow Rogers' checkpoint rules? Did any crash feel like a rock on the head?
- [ ] **Clash of Clans or Hay Day** (10 min) — Look for: Every timer on screen: building, production, boosts, events. → Which timers pace your session, which create appointments, which are pushed as skips, and do all show remaining time clearly?
- [ ] **Block Blast! or a word puzzle game** (10 min) — Look for: Goal clarity, hint systems, feedback and how failure is handled. → Does it show the door first, give hot/cold feedback, and keep hint costs mild and optional?

## 🏰 Level 9 — Everything I Learned about Level Design, I Learned from Level 9

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Mexican Pizza Generator** (10 min) — Write the 10 cliché themes on slips of paper. Draw two at random three times and pitch each combo in one sentence as a mobile game area or LiveOps event (e.g. sewer + pirate = a smuggler's flooded-tunnel event).
- [ ] **Squint Test Your Phone** (5 min) — Screenshot the home screen of 3 F2P games. Squint (or blur the image). Is the brightest thing the main action? Score each out of 10 and note what steals attention.
- [ ] **Weenie Hunt** (10 min) — In any F2P game, list every weenie: something visible but not yet reachable that makes you want to keep playing (locked area, top pass reward, next arena). How many are visible from the home screen?
- [ ] **Walking Audit** (10 min) — Play one session and time every second of non-interactive dead time (loads, pop-ups, unskippable animations). Total it and pick the first thing you'd cut.
- [ ] **Name Game Drill** (10 min) — Take one level concept (a lava factory). Write a file name, then functional, location, descriptive and punny player-facing names. Would the pun survive translation?
- [ ] **Finger Doodle** (15 min) — On graph paper draw a linear corridor (player = 1 square, passages 5 squares wide). Add 3 fingers, each with a different reward, plus a weenie and a legend.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The 30-Level Saw-Tooth Beat Chart** (90 min) — Design the first 30 levels of an original match-3 or block-puzzle game as a beat-chart spreadsheet. Columns = levels 1–30. Rows = level ID, goal type, moves, new element introduced, blockers present, target difficulty (easy/medium/hard/super hard), reward, area/theme, palette, monetization touchpoint, story beat. Rules: one new blocker at a time with an intro level; saw-tooth difficulty with relief after each spike; every element used at least 3 times; no back-to-back hard levels; at least 2 themed areas with a weenie teasing area 2. Then audit it like Relic Raider and fix what you find.
  - Deliverable: A spreadsheet beat chart plus a list of at least 5 self-found problems and fixes.
  - Done when: 30 columns and 10+ rows are filled, a visible row or sparkline shows the saw-tooth, 5+ issues are listed with fixes, and you've posted a screenshot publicly with one sentence on your biggest fix.
- [ ] **Paper Map → Graybox Arena** (120 min) — Design one survivor-like arena or runner segment on graph paper: scale (player = 1 square), full legend (start, enemy spawn zones with aggro circles, bread-crumb pickups, obstacles, special terrain), a weenie, 2+ rewarded fingers, one sphincter-twitcher moment, and an explicit alley-vs-island decision. Then graybox it (any free engine, or even a spreadsheet grid) and pace it out.
  - Deliverable: A labeled paper map, a graybox screenshot, and a timed pace-out with a predicted final length.
  - Done when: The legend is complete, you've written 3 sentences justifying alley vs island, and you have a pace-out time plus a ×2 length estimate.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **An ad-monetized casual game (e.g. Block Blast!, a hyper-casual chart-topper, or an Archero/Survivor.io-style hybrid)** (10 min) — Look for: Every ad moment: type (rewarded, interstitial, banner), trigger and reward. → Which ads did you choose, which were forced, and did any appear at a frustrating moment?

## 📜 Level 3 — Writing the Story

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Nine-Doodle Story** (10 min) — Draw the 9-step structure as stick figures for a match-3 event about saving a bakery, one doodle per step.
- [ ] **Triangle Audit** (10 min) — For 5 games on your phone, mark which corner (characters, activities, world) is weird. Find one that breaks the rule and judge whether it suffers.
- [ ] **Little Red Remix** (15 min) — Turn another fairy tale into a 5-beat game outline with exploration, inventory, gating, a mini-game and a boss.
- [ ] **Title Forge** (10 min) — Write one title of each type (literal, cool, punny, purple cow) for one idea, ideally 2–3 syllables, then search the stores for collisions.
- [ ] **Bomb Under the Table** (10 min) — Redesign a surprise moment from a game you know as suspense: what does the player see coming, and for how long?
- [ ] **Name Fit Test** (10 min) — Sketch or describe 3 characters, name them, and ask a friend whether each name fits the look.
- [ ] **Skipper Test** (15 min) — Play a story-meta mobile game skipping every dialog. Note whether you still understand the goal and what told you.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **Write a Light Meta-Narrative for an F2P Game** (150 min) — Pick a core loop. Write a one-page narrative bible (setting with one weird corner, main character with name, look, personality and 2+ motivations, a helper important to gameplay, a title of a chosen type). Write Chapter 1 as 9 beats tied to gameplay actions, 6 dialog popups of max 2 lines, and a cliffhanger leading into a LiveOps event.
  - Deliverable: Narrative bible, 9-beat chapter, 6 dialogs, cliffhanger
  - Done when: Every beat links to a gameplay action, the chapter makes sense with all dialogs skipped, a friend can retell it from environment changes alone, and the title passed a store search. Bonus: post Chapter 1 publicly.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Homescapes** (10 min) — Look for: The loop: level, stars, task, dialog, new task → How much story per minute of play do you get, and can you follow the plot while skipping dialogs?
- [ ] **Royal Match** (10 min) — Look for: Moments of visible danger to the king or nearly-complete areas → Where does the game use suspense rather than surprise?
- [ ] **Brawl Stars** (10 min) — Look for: 5 brawlers' looks, kits, animations and voice lines → How do look, kit and voice match each personality or profession? Which is most 'true to themselves'?
- [ ] **Marvel Snap, Pokémon GO or Monopoly GO!** (10 min) — Look for: Details that show deep knowledge of the license → Where did the team celebrate the license, and where did they make it their own?

## 🎬 Level 16 — Cutscenes, or No One's Gonna Watch 'Em Anyway

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Skip-o-meter** (10 min) — Time every story beat in the first 10 minutes of 2 F2P games. How many are skippable, and which ran over 10 seconds?
- [ ] **Do, don't watch** (10 min) — Redesign one cutscene idea ('the dragon burns the village') as 3 different playable beats on a phone.
- [ ] **Format drill** (15 min) — Write a 1-page mobile event intro scene in standard screenplay format (heading, camera, caps, parenthetical, brief dialogue).
- [ ] **Name That Tune** (10 min) — Write a 10-line dialogue exchange, cut it to 6 lines, then to 3 bubbles, without losing the point.
- [ ] **Grunt dictionary** (5 min) — Spell 10 reactions (light hit, heavy hit, fall, victory...) so an actor would perform each correctly.
- [ ] **VO sheet** (15 min) — Build a 15-line VO spreadsheet (File Name / Dialogue / Notes) for one gacha hero: summon, attack, skill, hit, KO, victory, with alternates.
- [ ] **Stage the tutorial** (10 min) — Sketch a tutorial moment on a phone screen and annotate how you'd guarantee the player sees it (focus overlay, camera pan, cause and effect).

**Main quests**

- [ ] **Story package for one LiveOps event** (120 min) — For a narrative casual game, write: a 5-beat event outline (start with a bang, finale on the last day); a beat sheet marking gameplay vs bubble vs short animated beat (each under ~10 seconds); one key scene in screenplay format; a text/VO spreadsheet with IDs, dialogue and notes; a 6-panel, 15-second ad storyboard that works muted.
  - Deliverable: An event story doc with outline, beat sheet, script page, line spreadsheet and ad storyboard.
  - Done when: Every beat answers 'could this be gameplay?'; no in-game beat exceeds ~10 seconds or 3 bubbles; every line has an ID and notes; dialogue was read aloud and revised; the storyboard is posted publicly asking 'would you skip this?'

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Homescapes or Gardenscapes** (10 min) — Look for: Story beats around your first tasks: length, bubbles vs animation. → How does the story give meaning to the next task, and how long does each beat take?
- [ ] **Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail or AFK Journey** (10 min) — Look for: Skip, auto-play and story log options; which moments get full cinematics. → Which scenes are 'kissing and explosions' cinematics, and which are plain dialogue?
- [ ] **Clash of Clans or Clash Royale (plus a Supercell trailer)** (10 min) — Look for: How the tutorial stages cause and effect; what the trailer shows. → What does the trailer do that the game itself never does?
- [ ] **Any F2P game's ads** (10 min) — Look for: Watch 5 ads muted: openings, real gameplay vs fake, captions. → Which ads start with a bang, which show real gameplay, and which would you skip?

## 🎵 Level 15 — Some Notes on Music (and Sound)

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Mute test** (10 min) — Play 3 levels of an F2P game muted, then 3 with sound. List 3 moments where sound added information and check whether a visual covered it too.
- [ ] **Speak musician** (10 min) — Describe a game's home-screen track using at least 6 of the 13 musical terms.
- [ ] **Sting collector** (10 min) — Note the level-complete, fail and chest-open sounds of 2 games. Which go up in pitch, which go down?
- [ ] **Leitmotif draft** (15 min) — Invent a hero and a villain for a mobile RPG and describe each one's motif (instrument, tempo, mood) and when it plays.
- [ ] **Priority sort** (10 min) — List 15 sounds in a match-3 or merge game and label each local, distant or priority.
- [ ] **Sweeten it** (10 min) — For 3 actions (gem merge, shield break, coin shower) propose a surprising real-world sound to sweeten each.
- [ ] **Naming pass** (5 min) — Write a sound naming convention and name 10 imaginary sounds for a LiveOps event.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **Audio Brief for a F2P mobile game** (90 min) — Write an audio brief: style and temp soundtrack (5–8 references with reasons), music needs list (every screen and event), dynamic themes, leitmotifs, SFX list by category plus mobile UI sounds, priority table, sound-off plan (visual + haptic twin for each priority sound), file-size budget and delivery plan, licensing checklist (including ads), naming convention.
  - Deliverable: A 2–4 page audio brief document.
  - Done when: A composer and a sound designer could start without a meeting; every priority sound has a visual twin; at least 8 musical terms are used correctly; it's shared publicly or with a musician friend for feedback.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Candy Crush Saga** (10 min) — Look for: How the announcer voice, pitch and music change as combos grow. → What stays readable with sound off, and what is only heard?
- [ ] **Clash Royale** (10 min) — Look for: Play 2 matches with sound and 1 muted; listen to overtime music. → What information did you lose when muted, and how did overtime music change your play?
- [ ] **Subway Surfers (or another endless runner)** (10 min) — Look for: Music tempo vs on-screen speed. → Does the music feel more exciting than the action, and does it make you play faster?
- [ ] **Any gacha or chest-opening game** (10 min) — Look for: The reveal sequence: build-up, silence, rarity sounds. → Where is silence or a build-up used, and how do sounds signal rarity before you read it?

## 🏁 Level 17 — And Now the Hard Part

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Twiddley-bit liposuction** (10 min) — Write everything about a game idea for 3 minutes, then cross out every twiddley bit until only 5 bullets remain, one per pitch basic (title, company, high concept, details, proof).
- [ ] **Lore detox** (5 min) — Describe your favorite F2P game's pitch in two sentences without a single proper noun.
- [ ] **Right-people research** (15 min) — Pick one mobile publisher or studio, list its last 5 games and genres, and finish: "They'd say yes to ____ and never to ____."
- [ ] **Pitch disaster drill** (10 min) — List 6 things that could go wrong in a video-call pitch and a backup for each. Then answer: could you pitch if the power went out?
- [ ] **Jenga audit** (10 min) — Pick one F2P feature (e.g., daily login rewards), draw every system it touches, and decide whether cutting it is safe or dangerous.
- [ ] **30/70 sorter** (10 min) — Compare a successor pair (Gardenscapes → Homescapes or Royal Match → Royal Kingdom) via store pages and videos. List what carried over (the 70) and what's new (the 30).
- [ ] **Chili recall** (5 min) — Closed-book: write the 7 chili ↔ game-dev mappings (recipe, ingredients, brown first, boil, simmer, next-day taste, ugly-but-tasty).

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The 10-slide F2P pitch** (90 min) — Build a 10–13 slide pitch deck for an original F2P concept: hook, audience, market fit with 3 comparables, core loop diagram, monetization and retention hypotheses, LiveOps plan, KPI targets with kill criteria, production specs. Rehearse it aloud twice, once on camera or to a friend.
  - Deliverable: A pitch deck plus a 1-line answer to the 3 hardest questions you expect.
  - Done when: Fits in 10 spoken minutes; no lore paragraphs; every slide has a visual; you can state your D7 target and what happens if you miss it without hesitating. Bonus: post one slide publicly for feedback.
- [ ] **Teardown + sequel-or-LiveOps memo** (90 min) — Write a 2-page teardown of a live F2P game (core loop, meta, FTUE, economy, monetization, LiveOps) ending with a memo: successor app or big LiveOps overhaul? Apply SPINE: the spine, the 30% you'd change, what must never be taken away, the name, the 5 new things.
  - Deliverable: A portfolio-ready 2-page teardown + memo with one diagram.
  - Done when: Each section has at least one concrete observation; the memo makes a clear call with 3 reasons and names the "too new" risk.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Clash Royale or Brawl Stars** (10 min) — Look for: What's new this season or event vs. what's unchanged. → Roughly what new-to-old ratio do you see, does it match 30/70, and what's the season's "back of the box" list?
- [ ] **Royal Match (then Royal Kingdom if available)** (10 min) — Look for: What the successor keeps (spine) and what it adds (characters, meta, hooks). → Which 3 things carried over unchanged, and what's the single biggest new hook?
- [ ] **Any top-10 grossing F2P game** (10 min) — Look for: The game's pitch hidden in its FTUE: the hook it shows first. → Write its one-line high concept. Did it lead with gameplay or lore?
- [ ] **A new or low-ranked F2P game** (10 min) — Look for: Signs of design blinders: elements the devs probably thought were "good enough." → Name 2 half-baked elements and the playtest you'd run to prove it.

## 🧰 Bonus Levels — The Designer's Toolkit — Bonus Levels 1–9

**Side quests**

- [ ] **Speed one-sheet** (15 min) — Fill the F2P one-sheet template for a game idea in 15 minutes. One screen max, no paragraph over 3 lines, 5–7 USPs, 3 comps that are successful + recent + famous.
- [ ] **Triple-roll generator** (10 min) — Pick a random story-theme family, a random environment and a random mobile mechanic. Write a one-line hook for three combos and circle the best.
- [ ] **Back of the store page** (10 min) — Write exactly five "game goals" for your concept. Cross out any that a top-10 game in your genre could also claim, and rewrite them.
- [ ] **Blocker sheet** (10 min) — Pick one obstacle from a match-3 game you know and fill the puzzle "blocker template": layers, behavior, how cleared, first level, tutorial text, VFX/SFX, analytics.
- [ ] **Farm Wars goes mobile** (15 min) — Draw the Farm Wars core loop as a 4-box diagram sized for 3-minute sessions, then mark which of its six DLC items become LiveOps events.
- [ ] **Event dictionary** (10 min) — Write 10 analytics events for your game's first session: event name, trigger, parameters (e.g., tutorial_step, level_complete, currency_earned).
- [ ] **Three-second hook** (10 min) — Storyboard an ad creative in three frames (seconds 0–1, 1–2, 2–3) that would stop a scroller and honestly show your core loop.
- [ ] **Doc-drift detective** (5 min) — List three differences between the Farm Wars one-sheet, ten-pager and pitch, then write the version note you'd add to each document.

**Main quests**

- [ ] **The F2P paper trail** (180 min) — For your own F2P mobile concept, produce the F2P one-sheet, the F2P ten-pager (all ten topics), one filled enemy template and one filled boss template (or a blocker sheet plus a chapter-boss level for a puzzle game).
  - Deliverable: One-sheet + ten-pager + enemy sheet + boss sheet (doc, wiki page or slides).
  - Done when: The one-sheet fits one page; every ten-pager topic has content or an honest TBD; the core loop is a diagram; each currency has at least 2 sources and 2 sinks; both templates include telegraphs, rewards and analytics events; you've posted the one-sheet publicly and asked one feedback question.
- [ ] **The 15-slide F2P pitch** (210 min) — Build the adapted F2P pitch deck (cover, team, hook, market, audience, specs, core loop, meta, goals, monetization, LiveOps, UA hook, target KPIs, comps, production plan, roadmap, ask) for the same concept.
  - Deliverable: A 12–17 slide deck plus a 10-minute rehearsal.
  - Done when: Every slide has a visual; the hook slide works without you talking; KPI targets are labeled as targets; the production slide has a timeline with at least two kill/go gates; you rehearsed it aloud in under 10 minutes and changed at least one slide after a friend's reaction.

**Play Labs**

- [ ] **Royal Match or Candy Crush Saga** (10 min) — Look for: Every obstacle/blocker you meet and the level where it first appears, plus how it's introduced. → Which of the book's console mechanics (conveyor, teleportal, two-stage breakable, slowing poison gas, locked door) does each blocker echo?
- [ ] **Archero or Survivor.io** (10 min) — Look for: One enemy type: its movement, attack telegraph, damage feedback and reward. → Fill the mobile enemy template from memory. How long is the telegraph, and how is it made readable on a small screen?
- [ ] **Any top-grossing game's store page** (10 min) — Look for: Title, subtitle, screenshots and captions, description opening, genre tags. → Reverse-engineer its one-sheet: which 5 "back of the box" goals does it sell, and in what order?
- [ ] **Monopoly GO! or Hay Day** (10 min) — Look for: Every live event, timer, limited offer and seasonal element visible from the home screen. → What would this game's 2026 "download strategy" slide (pitch slide 8) look like?
